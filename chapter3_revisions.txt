A3. DISCLOSE THE STROKE-WIDTH / TOLERANCE METHODOLOGY (CRITICAL FOR FAIRNESS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM:
  CLRerNet outputs polylines, not masks. To score it on the shared pixel metric
  the polylines are rasterized to a binary mask, and a stroke-width tolerance
  (dilating both prediction and ground truth before scoring) is applied. This is
  what raises CLRerNet CULane from ~0.16 (raw) to ~0.64. The chapter never states
  (a) the rasterization line width, (b) the tolerance used, or (c) whether the
  SAME tolerance was applied to YOLOPX, HybridNets, and SCNN.

FIX — add a sentence to the "Evaluation Subset and Output Standardization" or
"Core Evaluation Metrics" subsection, e.g.:

  "Because lane-line detectors (CLRerNet) output structured polylines rather than
  dense masks, each model's lane output is rendered to a binary lane mask at a
  fixed stroke width of W px, and a stroke-width tolerance of T px is applied
  symmetrically to both the predicted and reference masks before computing pixel-
  level TP/FP/FN. The same rendering width and tolerance are applied identically
  to all four models so that thin-line and dense-mask outputs are compared on
  geometric agreement rather than on raster width."

  Replace W and T with the actual values you used. Confirm in your pipeline that
  the same tolerance was applied to ALL models (not only CLRerNet). If raw
  (tolerance = 0) numbers are available, consider reporting them in an appendix
  for transparency.

RATIONALE:
  Without this, a reviewer cannot tell whether CLRerNet's lead is real or an
  artifact of being the only model given a tolerance. This is the single most
  likely point of attack on the comparison.

B6. CLARIFY THAT IoU AND F1 ARE NOT INDEPENDENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL:
  "When both metrics are calculated from the same binary representation, they are
  mathematically related and should be interpreted together rather than as
  conflicting indicators."

REVISED:
  "When both metrics are calculated from the same binary masks they are
  monotonically related (F1 = 2 x IoU / (1 + IoU)) and therefore carry the same
  ranking information; they are reported together for comparability with prior
  work but are not independent confirmations of one another. Precision and recall
  provide the independent diagnostic axis used to interpret each model's error
  pattern."

RATIONALE:
  Calling two deterministically-linked metrics both "primary" overstates the
  evidence. Stating the relationship explicitly is more rigorous and shifts the
  diagnostic weight onto precision/recall, which is where it belongs.
